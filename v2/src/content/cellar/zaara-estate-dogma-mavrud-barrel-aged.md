Sakar isn't a name most people outside Bulgaria will recognise, and that's exactly the appeal of a bottle like this — a small estate making a serious case for a region nobody's heard of yet.

## History

Zaara Estate sits on a hillside above the village of Glavan, in the Sakar Mountains of southeastern Bulgaria, close to the Turkish border. The vineyards were planted in 2015 and the estate — which combines a working winery with a small hotel and restaurant, unusual for this particular region — opened to visitors in 2018. Winemaking is credited to Italian oenologist Giovanni Colugnati working alongside Bulgarian winemaker Slavey Paunov, though it's worth being honest that Colugnati's wider reputation is something I could only find repeated in the estate's own materials rather than independently documented — his role here appears genuine, but his broader standing in Italian winemaking isn't something I can verify.

## Region & terroir

Sakar is a lesser-known Bulgarian wine region, its soils mostly brown forest earth over leached, nutrient-poor granite, under long, hot, dry summers. It's historically been Mavrud and Rubin — a Bulgarian cross of Mavrud and Nebbiolo — country, alongside Merlot and Syrah, rather than a region built around any one flagship indigenous grape the way Melnik or Asenovgrad are.

## The grape

Mavrud is Bulgaria's signature dark-skinned indigenous variety, late-ripening and thick-skinned, prized for producing some of the country's most age-worthy reds. It's a demanding grape to grow well — low-yielding, awkward in a wet year — but the reward is deep colour, high tannin, and real ageing potential when the fruit is handled with care.

## Winemaking

DOGMA is Zaara's flagship range, and this bottling leans into Mavrud's natural intensity rather than trying to soften it into something more generic — barrel ageing here is used to add structure and integration to the grape's characteristically powerful tannins, rather than to mask them.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Blackberry and mulberry, dense and dark-fruited, with an earthy, savoury undertone typical of well-made Mavrud.

**Palate:** Powerful, grippy tannins balanced by real fruit concentration, dark and brooding rather than bright.

**Finish:** Long and savoury, with the tannic structure carrying well past the initial fruit.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at cool room temperature, around 16–18°C, and decant if you can — Mavrud's tannins benefit from air. It's built for rich grilled or slow-cooked meat and firm, aged cheeses.

## Why it's in the cellar

This bottle is here as a bet on a region that hasn't made its name yet. It's in the cellar because some of the most interesting Bulgarian wine right now is coming from exactly the places without an established reputation to lean on.
