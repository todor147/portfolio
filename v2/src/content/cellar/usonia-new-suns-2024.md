Most wines named after a place are a promise about where the grapes grew. New Suns is more literal than most: it is the name of the vineyard the people making it actually farm, and the label is a sunburst to match.

## History

Usonia was founded in 2019 by Julia and Alex Alvarez-Perez, who met in 2012 while working in New York City restaurants and spent years as sommeliers and restaurant managers before turning to winemaking, by way of stints at wineries in Austria's Burgenland and Australia's Adelaide Hills. The name borrows "Usonia", Frank Lloyd Wright's word for a democratic American landscape free of Old World convention. New Suns is their home vineyard in Ovid, on the west side of Cayuga Lake, planted to Cabernet Franc and Riesling in 2005 by Kelley and Angela O'Neill and taken over by Usonia in 2021.

## Region & terroir

The vineyard sits less than half a mile from Cayuga Lake, one of the Finger Lakes whose deep water moderates the climate in a region where cold winters and a short season make ripening red grapes a real question. Usonia dropped herbicides and insecticides as soon as it took over, began farming a third of the site organically in 2024 alongside biodynamic preparations, and has farmed the whole vineyard organically since 2025. Since 2023, dead or dying vines have been replaced with inter-specific hybrids — Marquette and Noiret among the Cabernet Franc — though in 2024 the site was still roughly 80% vinifera.

## The grape

Cabernet Franc is increasingly billed as the Finger Lakes' flagship red. A parent of both Cabernet Sauvignon and Merlot, it buds and ripens at least a week earlier than Cabernet Sauvignon, which is why it ripens more reliably than most red vinifera in a cool climate. It tends toward red fruit, herbs and a savoury edge rather than weight, which is exactly the register this wine sits in.

## Winemaking

The fruit was hand-harvested in the second week of October, fully destemmed and given a gentle ten-day maceration. The wine then spent 15 months in neutral barrique, meaning oak that adds texture rather than flavour, and was bottled in spring 2026.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Cedar and raspberry, with a perfumed rose-petal lift.

**Palate:** Pure and elegant, the cooler vintage keeping the red fruit in front rather than weight.

**Finish:** Bright and unforced — in the estate's view, one to enjoy now rather than lay down.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at cellar temperature, around 14–16°C, or with a light chill on a warm day — lighter-bodied Cabernet Franc takes to it well. It suits roast chicken, duck, mushroom dishes and charcuterie, and it is light enough to work with salmon or grilled vegetables.

## Why it's in the cellar

This is the outlier on the shelf: the only bottle from the United States, from a young estate converting its vineyard to organic farming and making a case for cool-climate Cabernet Franc. It is also a wine you can trace to a single named vineyard, made by the people who farm it.
