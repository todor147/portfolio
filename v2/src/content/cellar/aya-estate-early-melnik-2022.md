Melnik 55 usually turns up in this cellar dark, oaked and serious. Aya picks it for freshness instead, and the result is a light, lifted red that a Decanter taster compared to Nebbiolo and Pinot Noir.

## History

Aya Estate is a young biodynamic winery in the Struma Valley, near the town of Melnik, with co-founder Dilyana Vasileva in charge of the winegrowing. Its first vintage, in 2015, was a small quantity of a single wine; the first commercial vintage followed in 2017. The estate farms 35 hectares at Harsovo plus a cool-climate Black Sea vineyard of nine hectares, first planted in 2014, and makes around 250,000 bottles a year. Its new winery was officially opened on 27 September 2025, just in time for that year's harvest.

## Region & terroir

The estate describes the Early Melnik vines as young plantings at Harsovo, close to Melnik in Bulgaria's south-west, not far from the Greek border. Harsovo is the warm site in Aya's portfolio — the cool one is on the Black Sea — which makes a fresh, early-picked red the less obvious choice here. The Struma Valley sits within the wider PGI Thracian Lowlands, Officially the wine falls under the broad PGI Thracian Lowlands, which covers the whole of southern Bulgaria, the Struma Valley included.

## The grape

Early Melnik is the name Aya uses for Melnik 55, a crossing of Shiroka Melnishka Loza (broad-leaved Melnik) and the French variety Valdiguié, bred to ripen earlier than its local parent while keeping some of its character. In this bottling the earlier ripening is put to work for freshness rather than power, which is why the wine is lighter than the grape's reputation for dark, structured reds would suggest.

## Winemaking

The grapes are picked by hand. According to the estate's notes for the 2022, the must is held cold for two days with daily pump-overs before fermentation starts, and fermentation runs at 14–23°C. Malolactic conversion happens in stainless steel, followed by eight months in old French barrels; the lot ran to 5,408 bottles. Decanter's taster found the oak deftly handled rather than obvious.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Sour cherry and slightly wild raspberry, with red plum, lingonberry and a hint of pepper; the estate's own notes add blackberry, spice and tobacco leaf.

**Palate:** Light to medium in body and bright, with firm, slightly grippy tannins and a savoury twist.

**Finish:** Fresh and focused, with a stony, tart-cherry edge that keeps it from feeling heavy.

## Serving & pairing

Serve slightly cool, around 14–16°C. Decanter suggested shepherd's pie or moussaka; the estate recommends roast red meat and, more unusually, light fruit tarts or cheesecake. It would also sit comfortably with mushroom dishes or hard cheese. Decanter's suggested drinking window runs from 2026 to 2028.

## Why it's in the cellar

Next to Villa Melnik's oak-aged AplauZ Melnik 55 Reserve and its sweet barrel-aged wine, this is the same grape in a third register — lifted, fresh and lighter on its feet. Three bottles of one variety, bred at a research station in the 1960s, show how much range it actually has.
