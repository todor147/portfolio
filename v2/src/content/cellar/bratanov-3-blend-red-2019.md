The big "3" on the label gives the wine its name, 3-Blend, and the blend does have three grapes, the third being the interesting one. This is a Bordeaux-style red in which a Bulgarian variety quietly does the job Petit Verdot would normally do.

## History

Bratanov is a family winery near the village of Shishmanovo, in the Harmanli area of South Sakar, run by a father and his sons with the stated aim of reviving a family winemaking tradition after a break of some fifty years. The first vines went in during the mid-2000s on reclaimed family land, and the winery opened in 2010. Maria Stoeva, a Dijon graduate, has been chief oenologist since 2015.

## Region & terroir

South Sakar is the south-eastern corner of Bulgaria, and the wine is labelled within the broader PGI Thracian Lowlands. The estate's vineyards are farmed without herbicides or pesticides, on sandy and loamy soils.

## The grapes

The 2019 is Merlot-led, at around 65 per cent, with about 25 per cent Cabernet Franc and 10 per cent Rubin. Merlot and Cabernet Franc are the familiar right-bank Bordeaux pairing; Rubin is the outlier, one of the Bulgarian varieties the family grows alongside the French classics, and it has been described as the spice in the blend. One Bulgarian retailer calls the wine Sakar's answer to the classic Bordeaux blend, with Rubin standing in for Petit Verdot. The proportions change from vintage to vintage.

## Winemaking

Bratanov has fermented all its wines with the wild yeasts of its own vineyards since 2016, and the 2019 follows suit: hand-harvested fruit, a fully spontaneous fermentation and no filtration. The importer's notes give around three years in oak — 37 months, spread across 63 different barrels — followed by a further spell in bottle before release.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Ripe black cherry, nutty and musky notes and a thread of dark chocolate.

**Palate:** Velvety, with plum, black cherry, tobacco and cocoa over a little vanilla from the oak, and a deep garnet colour in the glass.

**Finish:** Long, with ripe tannins and hints of tobacco and leather.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at around 15–18°C and decant for 30 minutes to an hour; as an unfiltered wine it may throw a little sediment. Red meats, hard cheeses and pasta with a rich sauce are the natural partners.

## Why it's in the cellar

It's a good test of whether a young winery in a little-known region can build a serious red from scratch. The blend is conventional on paper, but Rubin and wild yeast make it unmistakably Bulgarian, and the plain grey label with its single numeral is one of the more confident bits of design in the cellar.
