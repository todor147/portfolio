Symbiose was Bratanov's first series, and the name is meant to capture the bond between land, vines and generations; the bee-and-wildflower label makes the same point. The white is the easy-drinking way into the estate.

## History

Bratanov is a family winery near Shishmanovo in the Harmanli area of South Sakar, established in 2010 by a father and his sons. Symbiose was the first range the family released, and it now sits alongside the 3-Blend, Private Reserve and Vintage POP UP wines. Maria Stoeva has been chief oenologist since 2015.

## Region & terroir

South Sakar is the south-eastern corner of Bulgaria, and the wines carry the PGI Thracian Lowlands designation. Vineyards here are farmed without herbicides or pesticides, on sandy and loamy soils.

## The grapes

The blend shifts from vintage to vintage. The Belgian importer lists the 2025 as 60 per cent Chardonnay, 30 per cent Viognier and 10 per cent Tamianka, while a Bulgarian retailer gave the 2019 as Viognier, Tamianka and Chardonnay. Tamianka is the local aromatic variety whose revival in Bulgaria the estate helped lead with its 2013 single-vineyard bottling. Because the recipe moves with each harvest, treat those percentages as a guide rather than a fixed formula.

## Winemaking

Fermentation is with wild yeasts from the estate's own vineyard, as for every Bratanov wine since 2016. There is no oak: the importer's notes on the 2025 describe a dry wine aged on its lees in stainless steel, while a UK retailer found a very slight off-dry twist in the 2022.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Expressive and floral, with white peach, yellow stone fruit, pineapple and ripe citrus.

**Palate:** Juicy, soft and medium-bodied, with white peach, pear, apple and a little spice over medium acidity.

**Finish:** Clean and gently aromatic, with a soft minerality.

## Serving & pairing

Serve well chilled, around 8–10°C. It works as an aperitif or with goats' cheese salad, fish and seafood, smoked salmon, aromatic Thai dishes and chicken with herbs.

## Why it's in the cellar

It is the quiet starter in a lineup of heavier Bratanov reds: unoaked, aromatic and meant to be drunk young. The bee on the label, picked out in a touch of gold, makes it easy to remember.
