Every modern Melnik grape traces back to this one, and this bottle is the only chance to taste where the whole family actually started — grown the old way, on a plot small enough to walk across in a minute.

## History

Villa Melnik was founded by Nikola and Lyubka Zikatanov, with vineyards planted from around 2004 in the Struma Valley near Kapatovo. Alongside its modern varieties, the estate maintains a single heritage plot — just 0.1 hectares — of Shiroka Melnishka Loza grown the traditional way, a small act of preservation as much as a commercial wine.

## Region & terroir

The Struma River Valley is Bulgaria's warmest and most Mediterranean-influenced wine region, its long, stable autumns exactly what this naturally late-ripening grape has always needed. Historically it's grown as a low, unstaked bush vine, goblet-trained close to the ground rather than trained up onto wires — a method that, among other things, helps the fruit hold onto heat radiating off the soil.

## The grape

Shiroka Melnishka Loza — "the broad-leaved vine of Melnik" — is the ancestral variety behind an entire family of modern Bulgarian crosses. In a 1963 breeding programme at a research station in Sandanski, it was crossed with a handful of other varieties to create Melnik 55, Melnik 1300, Ruen, and Sandanski Misket, each aimed at retaining some of the original grape's character while fixing its main flaw: extremely late, unreliable ripening. On its own, Shiroka Melnishka Loza has never really left its home turf — genuine attempts to grow it successfully outside this small pocket of southwestern Bulgaria are said to have consistently failed, which makes a bottling like this a rare chance to taste the source material rather than one of its descendants.

## Winemaking

This wine comes from a single 0.1-hectare plot of goblet-trained, bush-grown vines, yielding only around 1,400 bottles a vintage — a scale closer to a garden than an industrial vineyard. Farming it this way, without modern trellising, is a direct continuation of how the grape has always been grown in this specific corner of Bulgaria.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Tobacco, dried herbs, and red cherry, with an earthy, old-vine complexity.

**Palate:** Naturally lighter in colour and body than its modern descendants, with firm tannin and good acidity carrying the fruit.

**Finish:** Medium-long, savoury, and rustic rather than polished — a genuinely different profile from any of the Melnik-family crosses bred from it.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at cool room temperature. It suits simple, rustic food — grilled meats, aged cheese, cured charcuterie — rather than anything too refined or delicate.

## Why it's in the cellar

This is about as close as a bottle gets to tasting a family tree's root. It's in the cellar because the crosses bred from this grape are more polished, but the original is the one actually worth understanding first.
