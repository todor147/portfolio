This is the same grape as the sweet barrel-aged bottle a few shelves over, taken in the opposite direction entirely — dry, structured, and built to show what Melnik 55 does when the goal is depth rather than sweetness.

## History

Villa Melnik was founded by Nikola and Lyubka Zikatanov, with vines planted around 2004 near Nikola's home village of Kapatovo in the Struma Valley. The gravity-flow winery followed in 2013, and the estate has grown since into one of Bulgaria's most decorated producers, landing among the World's 50 Best Vineyards in 2020. Earlier vintages of this wine have done well in competition: the 2021 took Gold at the Balkans International Wine Festival 2024, and the 2013 won Silver at Decanter 2015.

## Region & terroir

This bottling carries the PGI Melnik designation, with the vineyards around Harsovo, within the wider Struma River Valley — Bulgaria's warmest wine region, its climate pulled toward Mediterranean by air funnelling up through the Rupel Gorge from the Aegean. Long, stable, warm autumns here give late-ripening varieties the time they need to fully develop.

## The grape

Melnik 55 was bred in 1963 at a research station in Sandanski, a deliberate cross aimed at ripening earlier than its own parent, Shiroka Melnishka Loza, while retaining some of that grape's structure and character. Genetic analysis has confirmed Valdiguié, a French variety, as the other parent — a cleaner story than the original breeding record's pollen mixture of several French varieties, though you'll still see both repeated. In dry red form, it gives wines with real depth, spice, and ageing potential rather than the lighter, simpler character often expected of Bulgarian indigenous varieties.

## Winemaking

Villa Melnik gives the wine 18 months in Bulgarian oak barrels, a regime that has held steady across the 2019, 2020 and 2021 vintages. That long barrel time is what knits the grape's natural spice together with the structure that extended oak contact brings.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Black cherry and cassis liqueur, with smoked wood, dried meat and a resinous note.

**Palate:** Smooth, with soft acidity; reviewing the 2020, Wine Anorak found it supple and lively, with cherry and raspberry fruit.

**Finish:** Spicy and oak-led; on the 2020, Wine Anorak also noted a tangy, faintly bitter edge.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at cool room temperature, 15–18°C, as the producer suggests, and decant first. Villa Melnik pairs it with wild boar, partridge, pork in creamy sauces, lamb with tomato sauce, cheeses and pasta.

## Why it's in the cellar

Put next to the sweet Melnik 55 and Aya's light, early-picked Early Melnik in this same cellar, this bottle makes the case for the grape all on its own — proof that a variety bred at a research station some sixty years ago can carry a genuinely serious dry red as easily as a dessert wine.
