"Vintage POP UP" is a range name rather than a descriptor, and the bird on the label is the clue that this is the playful side of a serious winery. Inside is Syrah co-fermented with Viognier, the classic Northern Rhône trick.

## History

Bratanov is a family winery near Shishmanovo in the Harmanli area of South Sakar, established in 2010 by a father and his sons. Vintage POP UP sits alongside the estate's Symbiose, 3-Blend and Private Reserve wines, and is presented as a line that emerges from the character of a particular harvest — made only when nature allows or inspires it, so a given wine may appear just once rather than every year.

## Region & terroir

South Sakar is the south-eastern corner of Bulgaria, and the wine falls within the PGI Thracian Lowlands. The estate is farmed without herbicides or pesticides, on sandy and loamy soils; for the 2024, the Belgian importer places the fruit in the Datarla area, in vineyards worked without ploughing.

## The grapes

Syrah is joined by Viognier, the white grape that adds perfume; in 2024 the two came from neighbouring plots that ripened at the same time, and no proportions are published. A UK retailer describes the result as inspired by Côte-Rôtie, the Northern Rhône appellation where the two are traditionally fermented together.

## Winemaking

The two varieties are co-fermented spontaneously with wild yeast, in line with the estate's approach since 2016, and a UK retailer adds that whole bunches are used to keep freshness and aromatics. For the 2024 there was no oak: the wine spent eleven months in stainless steel and six in bottle, and went out unfiltered and unstabilised, so a little sediment is possible. These details are for the 2024 release; as a harvest-by-harvest line, other years may differ.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Ripe blackberry, cherry, plum and violets; other descriptions add black pepper.

**Palate:** Soft, juicy and velvety, with a subtle spicy accent; the Viognier brings peach and almond.

**Finish:** Long, with dark chocolate and cherry.

## Serving & pairing

The importer suggests serving it at 16–18°C. Poultry and lighter meats with fruity sauces, mushroom risotto, vegetarian dishes and semi-mature cheeses all suit it, and the 2024 is given a drinking window of 2026 to 2036.

## Why it's in the cellar

It is a reminder that a Bulgarian winery can make a lively, aromatic red in a Northern Rhône mould without taking itself too seriously. The label's bird, the lime-green top and the Viognier twist make it the cheerful one of the cellar's Bratanov bottles.
