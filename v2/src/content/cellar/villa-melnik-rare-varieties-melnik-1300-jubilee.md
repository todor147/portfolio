Some grapes get named after where they're from. This one is named after a whole country's history — a deliberate, symbolic naming that most bottles never get to carry.

## History

Villa Melnik was founded by Nikola and Lyubka Zikatanov, with vineyards planted from around 2004 near Kapatovo in the Struma Valley. Melnik 1300 itself predates the estate by decades: it was bred in 1963 at the same Sandanski research station responsible for the whole modern "Melnik family" of grapes, but wasn't formally named until 1981 — marking, as best as the naming can be traced, the 1,300th anniversary of the founding of the Bulgarian state, traditionally dated to 681 AD. It's a nice piece of symbolism for a grape bred specifically to carry Bulgarian winemaking forward.

## Region & terroir

The Struma River Valley's warm, Mediterranean-leaning climate and long, stable autumns give a variety like this the ripening time it needs to build real depth and colour before harvest.

## The grape

Melnik 1300 crosses Shiroka Melnishka Loza, the valley's pale, tannic ancestral grape, with Saperavi, a Georgian variety chosen specifically for its intense colour — Saperavi is a genuine teinturier grape, meaning its flesh is pigmented, not just its skin, which is exactly why wines made from it (and from crosses using it) tend toward such deep, saturated colour. That parentage is the whole logic of the cross: take Shiroka Melnishka Loza's character and structure, and fix its naturally light colour with a parent that has more pigment than almost any other variety around.

## Winemaking

Twelve months in new Bulgarian oak gives this wine time to integrate its naturally intense colour and fruit with real structure, unfiltered to preserve as much of that concentration as possible going into the bottle.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Blackberry, liquorice, and mocha, with a lifted note of fresh pomegranate cutting through the density.

**Palate:** Profoundly inky and full-bodied, dark fruit and chocolate carried by ripe, rounded tannins.

**Finish:** Long and rich, the pomegranate freshness returning at the end to keep the wine from feeling heavy.

## Serving & pairing

Serve at cool room temperature, decanted if possible to let the density open up. It pairs well with rich braised or grilled meat, game, or hard aged cheese.

## Why it's in the cellar

A grape named for a nation's history has a certain amount to live up to, and this one manages it. It's in the cellar as one of the more genuinely singular reds in the whole collection — a cross that could only exist in this one valley.
