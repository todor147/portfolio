A French sweet-wine grape, grown on Bulgaria's Black Sea coast and bottled under a gold-capped, sunburst label by a producer better known for Melnik reds from the opposite corner of the country.

## History

Disegno is the line Aya Estate keeps for what it calls elegant, multi-layered wines for special occasions, alongside its AYA, AYANO and COLORITO ranges. Aya itself is the biodynamic Struma Valley producer behind the Early Melnik also in this cellar, with a first commercial vintage in 2017. The range includes a Cabernet Franc from a 50-year-old vineyard and this late-harvest Petit Manseng, bottled in 500 ml.

## Region & terroir

The grapes come from Aya's cool-climate vineyard on the Black Sea coast, in the lands of the town of Kavarna in north-east Bulgaria, farmed biodynamically like the estate's Struma Valley vines. Wine Anorak puts the Petit Manseng at about a hectare, planted on black earth soils. The estate credits the constant sea breezes with slowing the rise in sugar and keeping the acidity fresh — exactly the balance a late-harvest wine depends on.

## The grape

Petit Manseng is at home in south-west France, where it underpins the sweet wines of Jurançon and Pacherenc du Vic-Bilh. Its small, thick-skinned berries can hang on the vine late into the autumn, sometimes until December, building sugar while holding on to unusually high acidity — which is what keeps a sweet wine from tasting heavy.

## Winemaking

The 2019 was picked by hand late in the season — the estate gives the last week of October, though Wine Anorak heard the first week of December — and fermented spontaneously in stainless steel for about 26 days. A quarter of the wine then spent three months in 500-litre oak barrels before being blended back, and it finished at 10.5% alcohol with around 70 g/L of residual sugar.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Golden-green when young, this bottle has deepened towards amber; ripe pear and quince, apricot and fresh grape, with honey and a gently spicy edge.

**Palate:** Rich but balanced, with melon, marmalade, tropical and citrus fruit and honey, plus herbs, a fine minty note and a mineral, slightly saline thread credited to the coastal site.

**Finish:** Clean and quietly spicy, the acidity carrying the sweetness through.

## Serving & pairing

Serve well chilled, around 8–10°C, in a small dessert-wine glass. The estate suggests creamy desserts, fruit tart and chocolate pastries; a cheese board works too, and blue cheese or foie gras would suit its acidity.

## Why it's in the cellar

Late-harvest Petit Manseng is rarely seen outside south-west France, let alone on the Black Sea coast. This half-litre ties two of the cellar's threads together: Aya's modern Bulgarian winemaking, and the sweet wines that sit alongside the Royal Tokaji and the Zantho Eiswein.
