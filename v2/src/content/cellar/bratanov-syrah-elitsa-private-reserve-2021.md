The 2021 arrives tied with twine, its story printed on a kraft-paper tag at the neck; earlier releases, like the 2017 pictured, wore a black-and-kraft tag instead. It is a single-vineyard Syrah, named for a daughter of the family, and built to be kept.

## History

Bratanov is a family winery near Shishmanovo in the Harmanli area of South Sakar, established in 2010 by a father and his sons to restore the family's winemaking tradition after a break of some fifty years. Several wines carry the names of the family's children — Syrah Elitsa, Chardonnay Beloslava and Rosé Maria — and Elitsa is the second daughter. The Belgian importer adds that she was born in 2011, the year of the estate's first Syrah harvest.

## Region & terroir

The grapes come from the family's vineyards around Shishmanovo in South Sakar, in the south-east of Bulgaria, and the wine is labelled PGI Thracian Lowlands. The estate farms without herbicides or pesticides, on sandy and loamy soils.

## The grape

Syrah is one of the French varieties the family planted alongside Bulgarian ones such as Rubin and Tamianka when the estate's first 24 hectares or so of vineyards went in between 2006 and 2008. Here it is bottled on its own, from a single vineyard, as one of the winery's Private Reserve wines.

## Winemaking

The grapes are picked by hand and fermented with wild yeasts, as every Bratanov wine has been since 2016. Earlier editions spent roughly 30 to 34 months in a selection of barrels of different sizes — French, Hungarian and Bulgarian oak — followed by several years in bottle; the 2017, for example, was released in January 2024. The wine is unfiltered and can carry fine lees. These figures describe the cuvée's usual pattern rather than this particular vintage.

## Tasting notes

**Nose:** Dark fruit with roasted notes; the 2017 edition was described with dark chocolate, mocha, cigar tobacco and spice.

**Palate:** Juicy and harmonious, with balanced acidity and subtle, silky tannins.

**Finish:** Fresh and lively, with a cocoa-powder edge in the 2017.

## Serving & pairing

Decant for 30 to 60 minutes and serve at around 15–18°C. Roast red meat, game, meat stews and aged cheeses are the suggested partners, and the 2017 edition was pitched as having ten years or more of ageing ahead of it.

## Why it's in the cellar

It's one of the family's most personal wines, and the one that shows what a young Bulgarian winery thinks Syrah can do when it is given years in oak and bottle. The tag and the twine make it look like a gift, which is about right.
